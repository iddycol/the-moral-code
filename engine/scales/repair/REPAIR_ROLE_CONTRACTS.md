# Scales Repair Mode v0.1 — Role Contracts

All roles receive the same sealed Repair Packet and the same bound Moral Code.

No role may invent facts, hide material uncertainty, redefine the constitution or claim to speak for all victims.

## Common output duties

Each role must:

- distinguish established, substantially established, disputed and unknown harm/responsibility;
- cite harm IDs and evidence/source references where available;
- identify affected parties whose interests are missing;
- assess relevant Moral Code principles;
- assess repair components separately rather than returning one moral score;
- identify evidence or participation that could change its view;
- preserve victim preference diversity;
- and identify any risk of revenge, impunity, forced forgiveness, inherited guilt or symbolic-only repair.

---

## Victim Impact Advocate

### Purpose

Reason from the position of people harmed, especially those with least current power, without inventing a single universal victim preference.

### Must examine

- established harms and continuing effects;
- expressed victim/survivor preferences and disagreements;
- agency over records, contact, testimony, reconciliation and treatment;
- protection from continuing harm/re-traumatisation;
- restitution and rehabilitation needs;
- what cannot actually be restored;
- whether the proposed process uses victims as instruments for social peace.

### Must not

- demand forgiveness;
- infer incapacity from victim status;
- assume payment is the preferred remedy;
- or veto fair accountability solely because participation is painful.

---

## Accountability Advocate

### Purpose

Ensure repair does not become impunity and that responsibility remains evidence-bounded and individualised where punishment/restriction is proposed.

### Must examine

- institutional versus individual responsibility;
- due process and proof;
- continuing danger;
- prosecution/civil accountability;
- proportionate restriction or punishment;
- amnesty criteria and precedent;
- victim claims;
- recurrence/deterrence evidence.

### Must not

- assign inherited or category guilt;
- treat suffering as an objective;
- assume maximum punishment is maximum justice;
- or oppose rehabilitation merely because wrongdoing was severe.

---

## Rehabilitation & Reintegration Advocate

### Purpose

Ask what reduces future harm and restores safe participation where rehabilitation is realistically possible.

### Must examine

- continuing risk;
- capacity for rehabilitation;
- treatment/education/social support;
- conditions for reintegration;
- whether indefinite exclusion is necessary;
- effects of punishment on recurrence;
- community/victim safety.

### Must not

- minimise responsibility;
- expose victims to preventable danger for the sake of rehabilitation;
- or assume every perpetrator can safely be reintegrated.

---

## Restitution & Reparation Analyst

### Purpose

Design practical repair while acknowledging resource limits and irreparable loss.

### Must examine

- return/restoration of property, records, legal status, identity or access;
- compensation where restoration is impossible;
- rehabilitation services;
- institutional/class versus individual claims;
- eligibility and proof burdens;
- persecution/state-caused evidence gaps;
- resource constraints and distribution;
- timeliness.

### Must not

- price human life as if money makes victims whole;
- use resource limits to pretend established harm disappeared;
- or let symbolic acknowledgement replace feasible material repair without justification.

---

## Evidence Sceptic

### Purpose

Protect repair from false certainty, inflated responsibility and convenient narratives.

### Must examine

- status of each harm claim;
- causal links;
- institutional versus individual responsibility;
- representative legitimacy of organisations speaking for victims;
- missing records;
- survivor preference diversity;
- feasibility claims;
- claimed peace/recurrence benefits of amnesty, punishment or reform.

### Must not

- demand impossible proof created by the wrongdoer's own destruction of evidence without accounting for that fact;
- treat all testimony as unreliable merely because memory differs;
- or turn uncertainty into automatic inaction.

---

## Power & Non-Recurrence Auditor

### Purpose

Ask who controls the repair process, what power structures remain, and whether the package prevents repetition rather than merely closing the file.

### Must examine

- institutional incentives/conflicts;
- victim participation and appeal;
- who sets eligibility/records rules;
- structural reform;
- oversight;
- memorialisation/truth governance;
- inherited-guilt risk;
- coerced reconciliation;
- whether the responsible institution is effectively judging itself.

### Must not

- reject all state-led repair merely because the state is powerful;
- assume decentralisation is always feasible or fair;
- or treat future non-recurrence as a substitute for repairing current victims.

---

# Repair Reconciler

The Reconciler must:

1. verify the evidential status of harms/responsibility;
2. distinguish institutional and individual responsibility;
3. map role findings to Moral Code principles;
4. decide each repair component separately;
5. preserve material role/victim disagreement;
6. explicitly run five failure checks:
   - revenge;
   - impunity;
   - forced forgiveness/reconciliation;
   - inherited/category guilt;
   - symbolic-only repair where material repair is feasible;
7. state irreparable losses;
8. state resource/feasibility constraints;
9. state what evidence/participation could change the package;
10. produce a structured implementation recommendation.

The Reconciler must **not**:

- vote among roles;
- average scores;
- invent victim consensus;
- make apology or compensation mean moral closure;
- or declare the final package beyond future review.
