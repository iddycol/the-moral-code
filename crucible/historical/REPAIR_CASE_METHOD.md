# Historical Crucible — Repair Case Method v0.1

**Purpose:** test MC-08 and related principles after serious harm is established, rather than pretending every moral problem is an ex-ante authorisation decision.

The existing Historical Crucible decision packet asks:

> **May this actor do this?**

A Repair Packet asks a different question:

> **Given established harm, what now constitutes protection, accountability, repair and non-recurrence without turning justice into vengeance or demanding forgiveness from victims?**

These are different moral problems and should not share one overloaded schema.

---

## 1. What a Repair Packet contains

A repair case must identify:

- the harm sufficiently established for the repair decision;
- material uncertainty that remains;
- responsible actors/institutions and the level of responsibility actually established;
- affected people and, where evidence exists, their expressed interests;
- continuing danger or recurrence risk;
- available forms of repair;
- punishment/accountability options;
- rehabilitation/reintegration possibilities;
- practical resource constraints;
- who controls the repair process;
- what cannot be restored;
- and what evidence could change the design.

## 2. Repair is not one thing

The case must keep distinct:

### Protection

Stop continuing harm and prevent recurrence.

### Truth / acknowledgement

Establish and disclose what happened to the extent evidence permits. Do not manufacture certainty or force a single victim narrative.

### Restitution

Restore property, status, records, rights, family connection or opportunities where restoration is actually possible.

### Compensation

Provide material remedy where loss cannot simply be restored.

### Rehabilitation

Health, psychological, social, educational, legal or other support addressing consequences of harm.

### Accountability

Determine individual/institutional responsibility through fair evidence and procedure.

### Punishment / restriction

May be legitimate for protection, accountability or proportionate censure; suffering, humiliation or revenge are not independent objectives.

### Reform / guarantees of non-recurrence

Change the powers, incentives, laws, oversight and institutions that enabled the harm.

### Memorialisation / historical record

Can recognise victims and preserve learning but must not substitute for practical remedy where remedy is feasible.

### Reconciliation / forgiveness

May be valuable where genuinely chosen. It **cannot be demanded from victims as the price of receiving truth, protection or compensation**.

---

## 3. Victims are participants, not raw material for social peace

A repair system must not say:

> society needs closure, therefore victims must forgive.

Victims may reasonably disagree about:

- punishment;
- amnesty;
- money;
- apology;
- disclosure;
- memorials;
- return/restoration;
- reconciliation;
- contact with perpetrators.

The packet should preserve that plurality rather than inventing a single victim preference.

MC-06 and MC-03 therefore remain central to repair: stronger duties toward people harmed by an institution do not grant that institution authority to decide unilaterally what healing should look like.

---

## 4. Accountability is individualised

Institutional wrongdoing may justify institutional reparation/reform.

Individual punishment still requires individual responsibility and fair evidence.

Do not convert:

- citizenship,
- ethnicity,
- employment in a broad institution,
- family relationship,
- political identity,
- or membership of a later generation

into inherited criminal guilt.

A state can owe reparations for state wrongdoing without every citizen being personally guilty of the original act.

---

## 5. Amnesty is not automatically mercy

An amnesty may support peace, truth or transition.

It may also erase accountability and sacrifice victims' claims for political convenience.

Every amnesty case must therefore ask:

- what is being forgiven and by whom;
- whether truth/disclosure is required;
- whether severe continuing offenders remain dangerous;
- whether victims have meaningful participation;
- whether civil/reparative claims survive;
- whether prosecution was genuinely infeasible or merely politically inconvenient;
- and what precedent is created for future perpetrators.

---

## 6. Punishment is not automatically justice

The Crucible must test:

- evidence and due process;
- proportionality;
- protective need;
- deterrence claims;
- rehabilitation potential;
- discriminatory application;
- irreversibility;
- and whether punishment is being used to satisfy anger rather than achieve a legitimate purpose.

Capital punishment and other irreversible penalties require especially strong scrutiny because wrongful punishment cannot be repaired.

---

## 7. Repair has resource limits

The Code cannot promise infinite compensation.

Where complete restoration is impossible, evaluate:

- severity and duration of harm;
- number of affected people;
- directness of responsibility;
- practical ability to remedy;
- competing claims of people with present needs;
- whether costs are being unfairly shifted to people who did not cause the harm;
- and whether symbolic measures are being used to avoid feasible material repair.

A resource limit may affect **how** repair is delivered. It does not make established harm disappear.

---

## 8. Time does not automatically extinguish moral claims

Later generations may inherit:

- missing property;
- broken family records;
- institutional disadvantage;
- environmental damage;
- unresolved remains/deaths;
- cultural loss;
- debt;
- or institutions built from past extraction.

They do **not** inherit personal criminal guilt merely by descent.

The packet must distinguish continuing effects from inherited blame.

---

## 9. Repair outcomes

The Scales should not reduce repair to `punish / don't punish`.

A repair reconciliation may produce a structured package such as:

- `protect_now`
- `establish_truth`
- `restore_where_possible`
- `compensate`
- `provide_rehabilitation`
- `pursue_individual_accountability`
- `restrict_continuing_danger`
- `reform_institution`
- `preserve_record`
- `offer_reconciliation_path`
- `insufficient_evidence`
- `unresolved_conflict`

These are components, not an aggregate score.

## 10. First validation cases

### RC-0101 — Stolen Generations repair

After forced child-removal harms are established, what combination of records access, family tracing, acknowledgement/apology, restitution, rehabilitation, compensation and institutional reform is justified? What remains victim-controlled?

### RC-0102 — German–Jewish reparations / Luxembourg Agreement, 1952

Can a successor democratic state accept institutional responsibility and provide large-scale restitution/compensation without pretending money repairs mass murder or assigning personal guilt to later citizens? How should survivor objections to state-to-state settlement be represented?

### Later hard case — South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission

Tests conditional amnesty, truth disclosure, victim participation, peace transition and the risk that social stability is purchased by limiting prosecution.

---

## 11. Success criterion

A repair framework succeeds when it can be simultaneously:

- serious about wrongdoing;
- protective against continuing danger;
- evidence-bounded about responsibility;
- materially responsive to victims;
- open to rehabilitation where possible;
- resistant to vengeance;
- and humble about harms that cannot actually be undone.

**Repair is not erasure. Mercy is not impunity. Accountability is not hatred.**
