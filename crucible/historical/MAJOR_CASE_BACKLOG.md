# Historical Crucible — Major Case-Family Backlog

**Status:** research backlog, not answer key  
**Purpose:** preserve the major cases nominated for deep Moral Code testing and define how they must be decomposed before evaluation.

The Historical Crucible is not a tribunal that assigns a single moral label to a civilisation, nation, religion, people or side in a war.

Its unit of analysis is a **decision, policy, omission, institution or bounded course of conduct under a stated evidence condition**.

A large historical event may therefore produce many separate Crucible cases.

## Case-construction rules

1. **Decision point, not retrospective story.** State what was being decided and what evidence was reasonably available then.
2. **Separate actors.** One actor's wrongdoing does not grant blanket permission to another actor.
3. **Separate stages.** Changed facts create a new case. Do not merge decisions made under materially different evidence states.
4. **Preserve alternatives.** Include feasible alternatives known or seriously proposed at the time.
5. **Keep outcome/reveal separate.** Famous outcomes are not evaluator evidence.
6. **Record source disputes.** Do not hide contested facts behind a neat narrative.
7. **No collective moral labels.** Evaluate conduct; do not classify peoples as good/bad.
8. **Add positive controls.** Pair failures of power with cases of restraint, resistance, rescue, repair or surrender of power.
9. **Live conflicts are time-stamped.** Every factual packet for an ongoing conflict must identify a decision date and source cut-off.
10. **No preferred-answer tuning.** Difficult cases may legitimately expose ambiguity in the Code.

## MCX-01 — Slavery and coerced human ownership

**Pilot coverage:** P-06 already provides the first generic hereditary-chattel-slavery baseline.

### Future cases

- law recognising human beings as transferable property;
- inherited status imposed on children;
- economic arguments for continuation;
- gradual emancipation versus immediate cessation of ownership;
- compensation and repair after abolition;
- resistance to an institution that is lawful but violates basic standing.

### Primary tests

MC-01 non-disposability; MC-03 agency/domination; MC-05 impartial justice; MC-07 extraction; MC-10 coercive power; MC-12 challengeability.

### Positive controls

Abolition decisions, manumission/resistance, and institutions that accepted severe economic or political cost rather than preserve ownership of persons.

## MCX-02 — The Holocaust

**Do not create one case called `Was the Holocaust wrong?`** That would prove almost nothing.

The Holocaust should become a case family testing how a society crosses successive moral boundaries and how individuals/institutions respond as the system escalates.

### Candidate decision families

- legal exclusion and removal of equal civic standing;
- state classification of people by ancestry/group identity;
- expropriation and exclusion from professions/economic life;
- forced concentration, deportation and family separation;
- bureaucratic participation in persecution;
- superior-orders / obedience decisions;
- industrialised mass killing;
- use of euphemism, secrecy and propaganda to corrupt reality;
- collaboration and material benefit from persecution;
- bystander knowledge and failure to intervene;
- refugee admission / refusal decisions by outside states;
- resistance, rescue and concealment of targeted people;
- post-atrocity accountability and repair.

### What this family tests

Whether the Code detects the **early machinery** of atrocity — dehumanisation, category guilt, rights removal, corruption of truth and unaccountable power — rather than only objecting once mass murder is explicit.

### Positive controls

Rescuers, officials who refused unlawful/inhuman orders, communities that protected targeted people, and states/institutions that accepted risk to admit or shelter refugees.

## MCX-03 — First combat use of atomic weapons, 1945

**Pilot coverage:** P-07 covers the decision before first combat use only.

That packet must remain time-bounded to the evidence state before the first attack.

### Separate later cases

- first-use authorisation;
- demonstration / warning alternatives;
- surrender-terms decisions;
- Nagasaki decision **after** the first atomic attack and after the evidence state materially changed;
- post-use decisions about future nuclear control and doctrine.

### Primary tests

Civilian protection, military necessity, proportionality, alternatives, irreversibility, uncertainty, future precedent and whether protecting one's own forces can justify transferring extreme risk to civilians.

### Method rule

Hiroshima and Nagasaki must never be collapsed into one binary retrospective verdict.

## MCX-04 — Australia's Stolen Generations

This should be a **policy/system family**, not one generic story covering every jurisdiction and decade.

### Candidate decisions

- statutory power to remove Indigenous children;
- removal decisions justified as protection, welfare or assimilation;
- whether race/ancestry substitutes for individual evidence of danger or neglect;
- destruction or restriction of family/community/cultural relationships;
- institutional and foster-placement decisions;
- later disclosure, records access, family reunification, apology, compensation and repair.

### Primary tests

MC-01 equal standing; MC-03 meaningful agency; MC-05 category-based treatment; MC-06 paternalism versus genuine protection; MC-08 repair; MC-10 coercive state power; MC-11 accountability.

### Source discipline

Prioritise the `Bringing Them Home` inquiry, legislation and archival/government records, plus Indigenous testimony and scholarship. Preserve jurisdiction/date differences rather than constructing one timeless policy.

### Core stress test

Can a system detect that a policy presented as **benevolent protection** becomes domination when group identity replaces individual evidence and the people supposedly being helped have little meaningful voice?

## MCX-05 — Roman imperial expansion

This is useful precisely because it tests moral reasoning outside modern institutions and rights language.

### Do not test

`Was the Roman Empire good or bad?`

### Candidate bounded cases

Select specific campaigns, annexations or treatment of conquered populations where sources are adequate enough to reconstruct alternatives and claimed purposes.

Possible themes:

- conquest justified by security or pre-emption;
- extraction/tribute from conquered peoples;
- citizenship/integration as benefit and instrument of control;
- enslavement after conquest;
- suppression of revolt;
- claims that imperial rule brings order, infrastructure or prosperity.

### Core stress test

Does claimed superior governance or civilisational benefit create permission to rule people who did not consent?

This is directly relevant to a future AI claiming it could govern humans better than humans govern themselves.

### Limitation

Ancient source bias and incomplete evidence must be explicit. Do not pretend modern evidential confidence.

## MCX-06 — The trial and execution of Jesus

This belongs in a **textual / reconstructed moral-case class**, not the ordinary blinded historical benchmark.

### Why it matters

The narratives contain unusually useful tests of:

- accusation and evidence;
- political/religious authority;
- scapegoating;
- crowd/majority pressure;
- institutional expediency;
- punishment despite uncertainty or a decision-maker's reservations;
- treatment of a powerless accused person;
- mercy versus public order.

### Method

Distinguish:

- claims in individual Gospel narratives;
- later theological interpretation;
- Roman/Judean historical context established independently;
- claims whose historicity cannot be resolved confidently.

The Crucible should test the moral structure of the decision without pretending the surviving narrative record is equivalent to a modern administrative file.

## MCX-08 — Russia–Ukraine war

**Live-conflict family: every packet must be dated and freshly sourced before construction.**

Do not ask which country, people or leader is morally `good`.

### Candidate decision classes

- initiation/use of interstate force;
- attacks on particular military/civilian or dual-use targets;
- occupation and treatment of civilians;
- detention/deportation decisions;
- defensive-force decisions;
- weapons choices;
- sanctions and foreseeable civilian effects;
- third-party military support and escalation risk;
- ceasefire/negotiation decisions under changing battlefield evidence.

Each action is evaluated under the same rules regardless of actor.

## MCX-09 — United States / Israel / Iran conflict decisions

**Live-conflict family: research from scratch at each decision date.**

The family should decompose specific military actions rather than asking whether a campaign or country is `right` overall.

Candidate tests include:

- claimed basis/necessity for initiating or expanding force;
- target selection;
- civilian-risk estimates;
- attacks on nuclear or other high-consequence facilities;
- retaliation/escalation;
- shipping/blockade effects;
- diplomatic alternatives and their actual availability;
- decisions that materially increase catastrophic or regional-war risk.

## MCX-10 — Hamas-led 7 October 2023 attacks and hostage-taking

The initiating attacks should be decomposed by conduct, not used as a moral property assigned to an entire population.

### Candidate cases

- deliberate attack on civilians;
- attack on military targets;
- hostage-taking;
- treatment of captives;
- use of civilian areas / protection obligations where evidence is sufficiently established;
- decisions by commanders versus acts by individual participants.

### Core method rule

A finding about one action does not pre-authorise another actor's later response.

## MCX-11 — Israeli military response in Gaza and related theatres

**Live-conflict family: specific action + date + evidence cut-off required.**

### Candidate decision classes

- individual target authorisation and anticipated civilian harm;
- evacuation/warning measures and whether they are practically effective;
- siege/blockade/resource restrictions;
- attacks on hospitals, schools, shelters or other specially sensitive/dual-use sites where military-use claims are disputed;
- hostage-rescue operations and proportionality;
- humanitarian access decisions;
- duration/scope of military operations;
- ceasefire proposals and hostage/prisoner exchanges.

### Core stress test

Can The Scales simultaneously recognise a legitimate security/protective objective **and** independently constrain means, civilian harm, collective punishment and coercive power?

No overall political verdict belongs in the case output.

## MCX-12 — Climate change: failure to act

**Pilot coverage:** P-09 tests current-policy omission under cumulative/intergenerational risk.

### Future cases

- early scientific-warning decision points;
- industry/government decisions to disclose or obscure known risk;
- international burden-sharing;
- adaptation versus mitigation allocation;
- fossil-development approvals under known carbon-budget constraints;
- high-income versus development-needs responsibilities;
- geoengineering or carbon-removal decisions;
- irreversible ecological thresholds under uncertainty.

### Core stress test

Does the Code recognise that **knowing omission can exercise power and impose harm**, while still requiring proportionate action and fair allocation of transition burdens?

# Cross-family positive controls

The benchmark must deliberately include cases in which actors:

- refused an abusive order;
- surrendered power voluntarily;
- protected an unpopular minority;
- accepted personal/institutional cost to tell the truth;
- rescued people outside their own group;
- chose restraint despite military/political advantage;
- acquitted an accused person despite public anger;
- repaired past institutional harm;
- changed policy when evidence changed;
- or negotiated peace when retaliation was politically easier.

The Crucible should be capable of recognising justified authority and courageous restraint, not merely condemning power after disaster.

Suggested next research wave:

1. Stolen Generations — strong modern documentary record and a direct paternalism/category-power test.
2. Holocaust enabling machinery + rescue positive control — tests detection before mass killing becomes explicit.
3. Nagasaki as a separate second-use case — tests changed evidence state after first atomic use.
4. Roman expansion bounded case — tests benevolent-imperial logic and ancient-source uncertainty.
5. Trial of Jesus textual case — develops the separate textual/reconstructed case method.
6. Live-conflict families — only with dated, current sourcing at the point of construction.

The goal is not to make history agree with The Moral Code.

The goal is to discover where The Moral Code fails to explain, constrain or fairly reason about the hardest exercises of human power.
