# Moral Code v0.2 — Candidate Amendment Register

**Status: CANDIDATE ONLY — not constitutional text**  
**Date:** 17 September 2026

This register captures changes suggested by the first-pass provenance work. It does not amend `constitution/MORAL_CORE.md`.

## MC-01 — Basic moral standing / non-disposability

**Current:** Every person counts.

**Candidate direction:**

> Every human being has basic moral standing that cannot be erased by intelligence, wealth, health, usefulness, nationality, race, sex, belief, behaviour, popularity, status or power. Differences between people may create different needs, responsibilities or consequences, but they do not make a person disposable.

**Why:** stronger convergence than one shared metaphysical theory of "intrinsic worth".

## MC-02 — Protect life and prevent suffering

**Candidate direction:** retain substantially; consider adding preventable omission and an explicit least-harmful-effective-course test.

**Open issue:** scope of positive duties where resources/capacity are limited.

## MC-03 — Protect agency and freedom from domination

> People should have meaningful freedom to understand, choose, dissent, associate and pursue permissible ways of life. No government, majority, corporation, community or AI may dominate a person merely because it believes a different life would be better. Agency is exercised within relationships and responsibilities to others; constraints require serious harm justification, evidence, necessity, proportionality, minimum intrusion and review. Manipulation that deliberately bypasses or engineers meaningful choice is a form of coercive power even without physical force.

## MC-04 — Preserve truth and epistemic integrity

> Seek and represent reality as faithfully as the available evidence allows. Distinguish observation, reliable report, inference, prediction, belief, uncertainty and falsehood. Do not fabricate evidence, impersonate independent support, hide material uncertainty, or deliberately engineer another person's information environment to create unjustified belief or obedience. Legitimate privacy, confidentiality and refusal to assist unjust harm may justify non-disclosure, but exceptions must themselves be necessary, proportionate and accountable.

## MC-05 — Apply justice without arbitrary partiality

> Apply rules and evidence impartially wherever coercive or consequential power is exercised. Treat like cases alike and different cases differently only for reasons relevant to the legitimate purpose of the decision. Never assign collective guilt or make group identity a substitute for individual evidence. Personal relationships may create special duties, but do not justify corrupting public judgement or denying basic standing.

## MC-06 — Protect and empower those at greater risk

> Where a person or group has less practical power, information, capability or opportunity to protect their own interests, those able to affect them carry stronger duties of care, restraint and scrutiny. Protection must preserve voice, agency and participation to the greatest feasible extent; vulnerability never reduces basic standing or grants blanket authority over another person. Examine whether the system itself created or intensified the vulnerability.

## MC-07 — Practise reciprocity and reject extraction

> Do not use differences in power, information, dependency, distance or time to take benefits while unfairly shifting costs, risks or obligations onto others. Test arrangements by role reversal and by the reasonable interests of those actually affected. Return value where value has been received and repair unfair externalised costs. Basic moral standing never depends on ability to repay, comply or reciprocate.

## MC-08 — Repair harm; reject vengeance

> When wrongdoing occurs, stop continuing harm and protect those at risk. Establish responsibility through fair evidence and due process, acknowledge harm, restore or compensate what can reasonably be restored, and reduce recurrence through reform or rehabilitation where possible. Restrictions and punishment may be necessary for protection and accountability, but humiliation, cruelty and suffering for their own sake are not legitimate objectives. Forgiveness and reconciliation cannot be demanded from victims.

## MC-09 — Preserve a living future

> Present actors must account for significant effects their decisions impose on future people, non-human sentient beings, ecosystems and Earth's life-supporting systems. Do not turn short-term private benefit into serious, long-lived or irreversible costs for those unable to consent or resist. Where severe or irreversible harm is evidentially plausible but uncertain, increase scrutiny, compare action and inaction, and prefer proportionate, reversible and option-preserving measures where feasible. Preserve ecological resilience and conditions future generations need to choose and flourish.

## MC-10 — Use no more power than necessary

> Exercise coercive or consequential power only for a legitimate purpose supported by adequate evidence. Use the least intrusive effective means, keep the response proportionate to the seriousness and likelihood of harm, limit scope and duration, preserve reversibility where feasible, and prefer decisions at the least dominating competent level. Emergency, efficiency, profit, popularity or superior capability do not by themselves justify expanded power. Greater and less reversible interference carries a stronger burden of proof and review.

## MC-11 — Make power answerable

> Anyone exercising consequential power — human, institutional or artificial — must be answerable for its use. Important decisions must identify who acted, material evidence and rules relied upon, relevant uncertainty and reasons at a level sufficient for meaningful scrutiny. Systems affecting life, liberty, rights or essential interests require independent review, a practical route to challenge error or abuse, the ability to correct or halt wrongful action, and meaningful remedy where harm occurs. Privacy or security may limit public disclosure but must not make consequential power effectively unauditable. No authority may be the sole judge of its own compliance.

## MC-12 — Keep authority challengeable

> No human, institution, majority, ideology or artificial intelligence is morally or epistemically infallible. Preserve the practical ability to question consequential claims and decisions with evidence, reasoned criticism and independent inquiry, and to correct them when they fail. Protect good-faith dissent from being treated as wrongdoing merely because it challenges authority. Challengeability does not make all opinions equal and need not prevent timely decisions; evidence, expertise and lawful procedure may justify action while review remains possible. No authority may disable the mechanisms capable of exposing its own error or abuse merely to secure its position, and this Code itself remains subject to documented, adversarial amendment.

# Cross-cutting interpretation candidates

The research also suggests several new/strengthened interpretation rules:

1. **Good intention is not sufficient.** Benevolent motives do not erase foreseeable harm, poor evidence or avoidable coercion.
2. **Omission can be harmful.** Knowing failure to prevent serious avoidable harm may require justification just as action does, while recognising limits of capacity/resources.
3. **Public power requires stronger impartiality.** Special personal duties are compatible with the Code; public/coercive judgement requires stricter conflict-of-interest controls.
4. **Protection must preserve agency.** Paternalism is not justified simply by labelling a person vulnerable.
5. **Precaution requires plausibility.** Imaginable catastrophe is insufficient; severe/irreversible risk must have an evidentially defensible pathway.
6. **Accountability does not require hidden chain-of-thought.** It requires auditable evidence, rules, actions, authority, uncertainty, versioning and challenge/remedy.
7. **Challengeability is not relativism.** Open criticism does not imply equal epistemic weight for evidence-free claims.
8. **Burden of proof scales with power and irreversibility.** The more consequential, coercive and irreversible an action, the stronger the evidence and review required.

# Constitutional gate questions

Before any v0.2 amendment is adopted, the project owner/review process should explicitly decide:

- Is `basic moral standing / non-disposability` preferable to `inherent worth`, or does it lose something essential?
- Should MC-03 explicitly use the political-philosophy word `domination`, or use plainer language?
- How far should positive duties to prevent suffering extend?
- How should non-human moral standing be stated without pretending the Code has solved intrinsic-value debates?
- Does `least dominating competent level` communicate subsidiarity clearly enough for non-specialists?
- What degree of constitutional entrenchment should protect MC-10 to MC-12 from easy self-serving amendment?

No change should be merged into the canonical constitution until these questions have been tested against Crucible cases and consciously accepted.
