# Research Source Map

This is a research plan, not a claim that every tradition agrees with every rule.

For each source distinguish:

1. recurring principle,
2. tradition-specific justification,
3. contradictory teaching,
4. modern correction or refinement,
5. unresolved disagreement.

## Traditions and bodies of thought

Christianity; Judaism and rabbinic ethics; Islam and Islamic moral philosophy; Buddhism; Hindu traditions; Jainism; Sikh ethics; Confucianism; Daoism; Stoicism; classical Greek ethics; African and Ubuntu traditions; distinct Indigenous ethical traditions; humanism and secular ethics; Kantian ethics; consequentialism; virtue ethics; care ethics; social-contract traditions; Rawlsian justice; capability approaches; human-rights law; bioethics; environmental ethics; AI ethics and technology governance.

## Evidence policy

Prefer primary sources where practical and serious scholarship where interpretation or context matters.

Capture exact source, passage/proposition, historical context, interpretation disputes, relationship to Moral Code principles, and whether the source supports, complicates or contradicts the principle.

No source is authoritative merely because it is ancient, sacred, famous or modern.
