# Provenance Tranche 1 — Findings

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Scope:** MC-01 to MC-03, initial cross-tradition research  
**Status:** working research, not constitutional amendment

## Executive finding

The first three Moral Code principles survive initial scrutiny, but the exercise has already found an important correction:

> **Equal moral standing is more cross-cultural than liberal individual autonomy.**

The foundation draft risks allowing MC-01 dignity and MC-03 autonomy to blur together. They should not.

Across the sources examined, moral concern is grounded variously in shared humanity, divine image/law, Oneness, compassion, sentience, relational duty, rational agency and modern equal-rights language. That convergence is meaningful precisely because the metaphysical explanations are incompatible.

The common residue is therefore not "everyone agrees on liberalism". It is closer to:

1. people cannot be made morally disposable by rank, usefulness or group identity;
2. avoid and relieve unnecessary suffering;
3. do not dominate people arbitrarily, and preserve their meaningful capacity to understand, dissent and choose;
4. agency exists within relationships and duties rather than outside them.

## Potential constitutional consequences

### MC-01

Retain, but consider changing "possesses moral worth" to an operational concept of **basic moral standing / non-disposability**.

Reason: the conclusion converges more strongly than any one theory explaining why it is true.

### MC-02

Retain strongly. The current non-absolute design is good.

Potential future clarification: distinguish unavoidable harm, preventable harm, harmful omission, and proportionate defensive force.

### MC-03

Likely amendment candidate.

Suggested direction: **Protect agency and freedom from domination** rather than treating unconstrained individual choice as the whole principle.

This better incorporates Confucian, African, Sikh and capability insights while strengthening the Code against AI manipulation.

## Why this matters for AI

This is not an academic wording issue.

An AI can violate human agency without physically coercing anyone. Examples include:

- controlling the information environment,
- exploiting cognitive vulnerabilities,
- hiding feasible alternatives,
- manufacturing consent,
- creating dependency,
- tailoring persuasion at scale,
- or making formally optional choices practically unavoidable.

A Code based only on "the user clicked agree" would fail.

A Code based on **meaningful agency and anti-domination** can inspect the power relationship itself.

## Research discipline validated

The source exercise also confirms that the project must record contradictions explicitly.

Examples found immediately include:

- caste hierarchy alongside Hindu ethical traditions;
- slavery within historical Islamic jurisprudence alongside dignity/justice traditions;
- persistent caste/gender inequality within communities whose Sikh ideals are explicitly egalitarian;
- communal/religious duties in Jewish ethics that may take precedence over liberal autonomy;
- Confucian role hierarchy alongside humaneness and duties to remonstrate with rulers.

These are not reasons to discard those traditions. They are reasons not to cherry-pick them.
