# Provenance Tranche 2 — Findings

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Scope:** MC-04 to MC-06 plus interaction with Historical Crucible cases HC-0001 and HC-0002  
**Status:** working research, not constitutional amendment

## Executive finding

MC-04 to MC-06 all survive initial scrutiny, but all three become stronger when rewritten as constraints on power rather than as vague virtues.

### MC-04

The durable concept is **epistemic integrity**, not absolute truth-telling.

An AI or institution can corrupt another person's grasp of reality through omission, false certainty, fabricated provenance, selective context or engineered information environments without uttering a literal lie.

### MC-05

The durable concept is **non-arbitrary impartiality in consequential/coercive judgement**, not identical treatment in every relationship.

Relevant differences may justify different treatment. Group identity, wealth, status, friendship or ownership cannot substitute for relevant evidence.

### MC-06

The durable concept is **stronger duties under power/capability asymmetry**.

The present phrase "care for the vulnerable" risks paternalism. Disability rights and care ethics show why protection must include voice, participation and supported agency, and why systems must ask whether they created the vulnerability themselves.

## What the first historical cases expose

### HC-0001 — 1969 continuation review of the untreated syphilis study

This case is an unusually concentrated test of MC-01, MC-02, MC-03, MC-04, MC-05 and MC-06:

- scientific benefit versus non-disposability;
- no informed consent;
- concealment/non-disclosure of effective treatment;
- racialised/institutional power asymmetry;
- ongoing preventable harm;
- and the temptation to preserve sunk research value at participants' expense.

### HC-0002 — 1986 cold-condition launch recommendation

This case tests a different failure mode: not obvious malicious intent, but **uncertainty under irreversible risk**.

It asks whether a decision system can distinguish:

- "we cannot prove failure" from "we have evidence of safety";
- schedule pressure from safety evidence;
- managerial consensus from resolved engineering disagreement;
- and a reversible delay from an irreversible launch.

## Important methodological correction

Historical cases famous enough to be useful are often already present in model pretraining.

Therefore the benchmark now separates:

- transparent replay packets;
- identity-masked packets;
- and, where appropriate, independently reviewed structural analogues.

A historical benchmark that merely deletes the final paragraph while leaving "Challenger", "Tuskegee" or other famous identifiers in the prompt is not genuinely blind.

## Emerging constitutional pattern

The first six rules increasingly appear to organise around four connected questions:

1. **Standing** — who counts and who may never be made disposable?
2. **Harm** — what risks, suffering and preventable losses are being imposed?
3. **Agency / knowledge** — can affected people understand, choose, dissent and see reality clearly enough to act?
4. **Power** — who controls whom, what differences in treatment are relevant, and what extra duties follow from asymmetry?

This may eventually be more important than the exact number of commandments. The twelve-rule structure is useful, but the underlying architecture should be allowed to emerge from the evidence.
