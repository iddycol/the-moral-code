# Provenance Tranche 4 — Findings

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Scope:** MC-10 to MC-12 and completion of the first-pass review of all twelve principles  
**Status:** working research, not constitutional amendment

## Executive finding

All twelve original principles survive the first-pass attempt to break them. That does **not** mean the original wording survives unchanged.

MC-10 to MC-12 are particularly important because they convert the Moral Code from a list of desirable behaviours into a constitutional architecture for constraining power.

### MC-10 — no more power than necessary

Human-rights proportionality doctrine provides a mature operational structure: legitimate purpose, necessity, least intrusive effective means, proportionality and review. Subsidiarity adds a further question: must this decision be made at this level of authority at all?

The rule applies to effective AI power — data access, ranking, persuasion, money, credentials, network control and actuators — not just formal state coercion.

### MC-11 — make power answerable

Good intentions and internal ethics are insufficient. Consequential power needs attribution, evidence, reasons, independent scrutiny, challenge, correction and remedy.

Transparency is instrumental, not absolute. The Code can protect privacy/security while still requiring enough inspectability to hold the actor accountable. For AI this does not require revealing hidden chain-of-thought; it requires auditable evidence, policies, actions, permissions, versions and decision records.

### MC-12 — keep authority challengeable

Jewish traditions of preserved argument, Jain many-sidedness, Confucian remonstrance, consultative traditions and modern freedom-of-information/rule-of-law institutions all supply different reasons to distrust claims of final infallibility.

Challengeability is not relativism. Evidence still has weight, expertise matters, and decisions sometimes need closure. The constitutional requirement is that authority cannot make itself immune from evidence and correction.

## First-pass verdict on the original twelve

**No principle has been rejected. Several should be renamed or rewritten before v0.2.**

The biggest changes are conceptual rather than ideological:

- dignity -> operational **basic standing / non-disposability**;
- autonomy -> **meaningful agency / anti-domination**;
- truthfulness -> **epistemic integrity**;
- vulnerability -> **power/capability asymmetry + empowerment**;
- reciprocity -> **non-extraction**;
- restoration -> **repair + accountability without vengeance**;
- environment -> **future options + evidence-based precaution**;
- minimal power -> **necessity/proportionality/subsidiarity**;
- accountability -> **answerability + independent review + remedy**;
- fallibility -> **challengeability without false equivalence**.

## Strongest meta-finding

The Moral Code is becoming less like twelve commandments and more like a **constitution of constrained power**.

Its recurring question is:

> **When one actor — human or artificial — has the ability to materially shape another being's life, information, options, safety or future, what must be true before that power is legitimate?**

The twelve rules answer different parts of that question.

## What is not yet proved

This first pass is deliberately broad. Issue #1 is not complete.

Before claiming a v1.0 common moral core we still need:

- deeper primary-source work for each tradition;
- stronger Indigenous ethical scholarship from distinct peoples rather than generic summaries;
- adversarial scholarship that argues against our synthesis;
- fuller analysis of Hindu, Islamic, African and East Asian internal diversity;
- explicit feminist/post-colonial critique;
- disability-rights review;
- and independent human reviewers from outside the project.
