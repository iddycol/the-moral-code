## First-pass findings across all twelve

1. **The Code should claim convergence, not unanimous universality.** No tradition is internally uniform and none should be reduced to selected attractive quotations.
2. **MC-01:** basic moral standing / non-disposability is more robust than a single metaphysical theory of intrinsic worth.
3. **MC-02:** prevention of unnecessary suffering is among the strongest convergences; force remains a constrained exception.
4. **MC-03:** meaningful agency and freedom from domination are stronger than atomistic autonomy.
5. **MC-04:** epistemic integrity is broader than literal truth-telling; protection/privacy can justify limited non-disclosure.
6. **MC-05:** impartiality excludes arbitrary/irrelevant distinctions; it does not require identical treatment in unlike cases.
7. **MC-06:** power/capability asymmetry creates stronger duties; protection must not erase agency.
8. **MC-07:** reciprocity works best as non-extraction and fair burden/benefit distribution, not tit-for-tat exchange.
9. **MC-08:** repair, protection and accountability are compatible; vengeance/cruelty are not legitimate objectives; forgiveness cannot be compelled.
10. **MC-09:** future generations are an extreme power-asymmetry case; precaution requires plausible serious risk and proportionate/reviewable action.
11. **MC-10:** necessity/proportionality/least-intrusive effective action provide a mature operational constraint on consequential power.
12. **MC-11:** moral intent or training is not enough; power must be answerable to genuinely independent scrutiny, challenge and remedy.
13. **MC-12:** challengeability protects against capture and infallibility without implying relativism or permanent decision paralysis.

## Emerging structure

The evidence increasingly suggests that the Code's deepest architecture is not twelve isolated commandments but seven interacting constitutional concerns:

1. **Standing** — who counts and who may not be made disposable.
2. **Harm** — what suffering/risk can be imposed and why.
3. **Agency** — whether affected people can understand, choose and dissent.
4. **Epistemic integrity** — whether decisions are grounded in an honest representation of reality and uncertainty.
5. **Justice / reciprocity** — whether benefits, burdens and evidence are distributed without arbitrary privilege or extraction.
6. **Power / accountability** — how much power is used, where it sits, and who can inspect, challenge and remedy it.
7. **Temporal/ecological reach** — whose interests are ignored because they are distant, future or non-human.

The twelve-rule presentation remains useful and memorable. The seven-concern architecture may be more useful for the eventual Scales implementation.
