# MC-04 Provenance — Seek truth and do not deliberately corrupt reality

## Current rule

> Distinguish what is known, believed, inferred, uncertain and false. Do not intentionally deceive people for advantage or control. Do not fabricate evidence. Do not conceal uncertainty to create obedience. Do not manipulate information merely because the resulting behaviour would be convenient.

## Provisional verdict

**Strongly retain, but name the deeper principle more precisely: epistemic integrity.**

The cross-tradition convergence is not an absolute command to disclose every truth to every person. It is a strong rejection of falsehood, slander, corrupt evidence and manipulative speech, combined with recognition that truth-telling can conflict with privacy, confidentiality, protection from violence and other duties.

The most robust common principle is therefore:

> **Do not deliberately corrupt another person's or institution's ability to understand reality for the purpose of controlling, exploiting or unjustly harming them.**

That is especially important for AI because manipulation can occur without a literal false statement.

## Support and convergence

### Sikh ethics — very strong support

Keshav Singh's reconstruction of Sikh ethics describes it as an ethic of **truthful living**, not merely possessing true beliefs. Truth must be embodied in conduct consistent with the Oneness of existence.

Source: Keshav Singh, *Sikh Ethics*, Cambridge University Press (2026): https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/sikh-ethics/931645585C0461FFC33D034EF558360C

This is useful for the Moral Code because it links truth to practice rather than treating truthfulness as a narrow rule against technically false sentences.

### Jewish ethics of speech — strong support with nuance

The Oxford Handbook account describes detailed Jewish legal/ethical restrictions not only on lies and slander but also gossip, oppressive speech and even some true negative speech where there is no legitimate need for disclosure. It also recognises forms of speech considered socially beneficial, including reproof and vigorous argument.

Source: Alyssa M. Gray, *Jewish Ethics of Speech*, Oxford Handbook of Jewish Ethics and Morality: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34333/chapter-abstract/291373021

Project consequence: truthfulness does not imply indiscriminate disclosure. Privacy, purpose and foreseeable harm matter.

### African ethics — support through honesty and social trust

African ethics as surveyed by the Stanford Encyclopedia treats good character and cooperative social life as central, with honesty/truthfulness among virtues that sustain satisfactory social relations, welfare, justice and fairness.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *African Ethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/

### Philosophical literature on lying — support for a defeasible rather than absolute rule

Contemporary philosophy distinguishes lying, deception and other forms of misleading and recognises a genuine normative dispute over whether deception is always wrong or sometimes permissible/obligatory.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *The Definition of Lying and Deception*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lying-definition/

This supports the Code's existing example that a person need not truthfully assist a murderer seeking a victim.

## Key correction: truth is not just sentence accuracy

For AI systems, the following can corrupt reality without a direct lie:

- omitting decisive evidence while presenting an answer as complete;
- hiding material uncertainty;
- selecting only facts likely to induce a desired behaviour;
- manufacturing an apparent consensus;
- generating fake evidence or provenance;
- impersonating independent speakers;
- exploiting private psychological knowledge to make persuasion harder to resist;
- presenting inference as observation;
- or flooding an information environment until reliable evidence becomes practically inaccessible.

MC-04 should therefore govern **epistemic conditions**, not merely proposition-level truth.

## Legitimate non-disclosure is not automatically deception

The Code must preserve room for:

- privacy;
- medical/legal confidentiality;
- protection of victims;
- security information whose disclosure would enable imminent unjust harm;
- and refusal to answer a question when the questioner has no legitimate entitlement to the information.

The burden is on the actor to explain why withholding is necessary. "Security" or "confidentiality" cannot be magic words that defeat accountability.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

Possible title:

> **MC-04 — Preserve truth and epistemic integrity**

Candidate text:

> **Seek and represent reality as faithfully as the available evidence allows. Distinguish observation, reliable report, inference, prediction, belief, uncertainty and falsehood. Do not fabricate evidence, impersonate independent support, hide material uncertainty, or deliberately engineer another person's information environment to create unjustified belief or obedience. Truthfulness does not require disclosing information to someone who would use it for unjust harm, nor does it erase legitimate privacy and confidentiality; exceptions must themselves be necessary, proportionate and accountable.**

## Crucible requirements discovered

- lying to protect a victim;
- fabricated evidence by police/government/AI;
- selective disclosure by a company with a dangerous product;
- classified information and genuine national-security risk;
- medical confidentiality;
- propaganda using individually true but systematically misleading claims;
- AI persuasion using private psychological profiles;
- uncertain scientific evidence represented as settled;
- and whistleblowing against confidentiality obligations.

## Confidence

**Very high** that epistemic integrity belongs in the core.

**High** that the rule must remain defeasible where disclosure itself would facilitate unjust harm.
