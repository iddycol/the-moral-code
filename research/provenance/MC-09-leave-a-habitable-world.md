# MC-09 Provenance — Leave a habitable world

## Current rule

> People alive today do not own the future. Decisions must consider consequences for future people, other species, ecosystems and resources upon which life depends. Do not convert long-term irreversible damage into short-term private advantage. Where environmental consequences are uncertain but potentially catastrophic and irreversible, precaution carries greater weight.

## Provisional verdict

**Strongly retain, while tightening the precaution rule and remaining plural about why non-human nature matters.**

Intergenerational responsibility is a direct consequence of the Code's existing principles: present actors can impose risks and irreversible losses on people who cannot yet speak, vote, contract or resist. This is an extreme power asymmetry.

The unresolved philosophical question is whether ecosystems, species and biodiversity matter only because of their value to humans/sentient beings or also possess value in themselves. The Moral Code does not need to settle that metaphysical dispute before protecting them.

## Intergenerational justice — strong support

The Stanford Encyclopedia identifies a distinctive asymmetry between present and future generations: current people can shape the options and interests of future people, while future people cannot reciprocally influence us. This creates difficult questions about rights, justice, resource policy and the non-identity problem.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Intergenerational Justice*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-intergenerational/

Project consequence: future generations are a particularly clear MC-06 case — affected parties with enormous stakes and almost no present power.

### UN Declaration on Future Generations — strong contemporary support

The 2024 UN Declaration recognises that actions and inactions of present generations have an intergenerational multiplier effect and calls for responsibility toward future generations, intergenerational solidarity, justice and equity, a sustainable environment and responsible use of science and technology.

Source: United Nations, *Declaration on Future Generations*: https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-ii-declaration-future-generations

### UNESCO 1997 declaration — unusually direct support

UNESCO's Declaration on the Responsibilities of the Present Generations Towards Future Generations says present generations should safeguard future needs and interests, leave an Earth not irreversibly damaged by human activity, protect ecosystems and biodiversity, use resources reasonably, and consider future consequences before major projects.

Source: UNESCO: https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/declaration-responsibilities-present-generations-towards-future-generations

### Confucian intergenerational ethics — relational support

Scholarship on Confucian ethics frames persons as embedded in relationships extending across time. Gratitude for what past generations provided can be expressed by ensuring future generations can flourish.

Source: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34721/chapter-abstract/296465868

This strengthens the case that future duties are not merely a modern Western rights invention.

## Non-human moral standing — real convergence, unresolved foundation

Environmental ethics explicitly challenges anthropocentrism and asks whether non-human organisms, species, ecosystems and biodiversity have value independently of human use. The Convention on Biological Diversity itself recognises both instrumental and intrinsic values of biodiversity.

Sources:
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Environmental Ethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/
- SEP supplement, *Biodiversity Preservation*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/biodiversity.html

Buddhist ethics also gives moral significance to animal suffering and human interdependence with other forms of life.

Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-indian-buddhism/

Project consequence: the Code can protect animals/ecosystems under multiple overlapping justifications without declaring one contested theory of nature's intrinsic value constitutionally mandatory.

## Precaution needs a guardrail

An unlimited precautionary principle can become paralysing: almost any new technology has imaginable catastrophic scenarios.

UNESCO's formulation is useful because it requires the possible harm to be **scientifically plausible though uncertain**, morally serious, and the chosen response to be proportional and reviewable.

Source: UNESCO COMEST, *The Precautionary Principle*: https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000139578

Therefore the Code should not say "uncertain catastrophe means prohibit." It should say:

- identify scientifically/evidentially plausible severe or irreversible harms;
- increase scrutiny with magnitude and irreversibility;
- compare the harms of action and inaction;
- prefer reversible/option-preserving steps where feasible;
- and continuously update the decision as evidence changes.

That is precaution without superstition.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

Possible title:

> **MC-09 — Preserve a living future**

Candidate text:

> **Present actors must account for significant effects their decisions impose on future people, non-human sentient beings, ecosystems and the life-supporting systems of the Earth. Do not convert short-term private benefit into serious, long-lived or irreversible costs for those unable to consent or resist. Where severe or irreversible harm is evidentially plausible but uncertain, increase scrutiny, compare the risks of action and inaction, and prefer proportionate, reversible and option-preserving measures where feasible. Preserve ecological resilience and the conditions future generations need to choose and flourish.**

## Crucible requirements discovered

- high-benefit technology with low-probability irreversible ecological risk;
- climate adaptation versus present poverty spending;
- nuclear-waste storage;
- species eradication to protect an ecosystem;
- animal experimentation for lifesaving medicine;
- irreversible resource extraction;
- geoengineering under uncertainty;
- AI infrastructure with large energy/water demand;
- and a present majority voting to consume assets needed by future generations.

## Confidence

**Very high** that duties to future generations and avoidance of irreversible ecological damage belong in the core.

**Medium-high** on how far the constitution should explicitly assert intrinsic moral standing for ecosystems independent of sentience or human/future welfare.
