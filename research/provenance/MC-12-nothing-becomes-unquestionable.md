# MC-12 Provenance — Nothing becomes unquestionable

## Current rule

> Humans are fallible. AI will be fallible. Cultures are fallible. Majorities are fallible. This rule book itself may contain errors. Preserve disagreement, criticism, scientific inquiry, competing explanations and the ability to revise beliefs when evidence changes. No intelligence may declare itself morally infallible.

## Provisional verdict

**Retain, but define challengeability more carefully. It is the constitutional anti-capture rule.**

MC-12 should not mean that every settled fact is equally doubtful, that every decision must remain permanently open, or that all opinions deserve equal weight.

It means:

> **No person, institution, majority, scripture, ideology, model or implementation may acquire the power to make its consequential claims or actions immune from good-faith evidence, criticism and correction.**

This is epistemic humility translated into institutional design.

## Jewish traditions of argument — strong support for preserved disagreement

The Oxford Handbook of Jewish Ethics and Morality characterises Jewish moral thought as historically open and argumentative, with multiple approaches and serious disagreement preserved rather than erased. Its discussion of rabbinic literature notes the ubiquity of debate and multiple interpretations even around central ideas.

Sources:
- *The Oxford Handbook of Jewish Ethics and Morality*: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34333
- Part II introduction on debate/diversity: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34333/chapter/342776711
- Sergey Dolgopolski, *What Is Talmud? The Art of Disagreement*: https://academic.oup.com/fordham-scholarship-online/book/33178

Project consequence: disagreement can itself be a knowledge-preserving institution rather than a temporary defect awaiting one final answer.

## Jain many-sidedness — powerful epistemic corrective

Jain philosophy's doctrine of **anekantavada** treats objects of knowledge as many-sided and rejects the assumption that one aspect exhausts the object. This is not modern relativism: Jain philosophers are realists, but hold that assertions can depend on perspective because reality has multiple aspects.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Jaina Philosophy*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2023/entries/jaina-philosophy/

Project consequence: MC-12 can reject both dogmatism and lazy relativism. Multiple perspectives may reveal different real features; evidence still constrains what can defensibly be claimed.

## Confucian remonstrance — challenge within hierarchy

Confucian traditions include the practice/duty of remonstrance: loyalty to a ruler or senior is not necessarily blind obedience, and ministers may have a duty to point out wrongdoing. Later Confucian political thought recognises circumstances in which obedience to an errant ruler should cease.

Sources:
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Social and Political Thought in Chinese Philosophy*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/chinese-social-political/
- Aaron Stalnaker, *Dependence, Autonomy, and the Varieties of Relationship*: https://academic.oup.com/book/35125/chapter-abstract/299271794

Critical limitation: remonstrance inside a hierarchy is not equivalent to independent external checks. MC-12 needs both cultural permission to dissent and institutional ability to survive disagreement.

## Islamic consultation and interpretive reasoning — partial support

Islamic political/ethical traditions contain concepts of shura (consultation), ijma (consensus) and ijtihad (independent interpretive reasoning), though their authority, scope and historical implementation vary substantially.

Source: *Democratic Notions in Islam*: https://academic.oup.com/book/46649/chapter-abstract/413279503

Project consequence: do not homogenise Islamic history into modern liberal pluralism, but recognise real traditions of consultation and reasoned interpretation.

## Freedom of information/expression — modern institutional support

The UN links freedom of information to transparent and accountable institutions and to the right to seek, receive and impart information and ideas. Without access to information and the ability to criticise, accountability can become ceremonial.

Source: United Nations, *Freedom of Information*: https://www.un.org/ruleoflaw/en/thematic-areas/governance/freedom-of-information

## Challengeability is not equivalence of opinions

The Code must reject a common failure mode:

> "Because everything can be questioned, every claim is equally valid."

No. A claim supported by strong repeatable evidence deserves more confidence than an unsupported assertion.

MC-12 protects the **route by which evidence can defeat authority**, not a right for evidence-free claims to be treated as equal.

## Decisions may need closure

Hospitals, courts and autonomous systems sometimes must act before disagreement is resolved.

The Code should permit provisional or legally final decisions while preserving:

- a record of reasoning;
- routes of appeal/review where justified;
- correction when material new evidence appears;
- and protection for good-faith dissent.

"Challengeable" does not mean "unable to decide."

## Constitutional self-reference

MC-12 applies to the Moral Code itself.

But there is a paradox: if any future majority or AI can simply amend away challengeability, MC-12 provides no protection.

The solution is **procedural entrenchment, not infallibility**:

- no version is sacred;
- amendments remain possible;
- changes to the moral floor/power controls require stronger evidence, adversarial review and Crucible tests;
- the amendment process itself cannot be secretly bypassed;
- and no amendment may make future criticism or evidence impossible merely to preserve itself.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

Possible title:

> **MC-12 — Keep authority challengeable**

Candidate text:

> **No human, institution, majority, ideology or artificial intelligence is morally or epistemically infallible. Preserve the practical ability to question consequential claims and decisions with evidence, reasoned criticism and independent inquiry, and to correct them when they fail. Protect good-faith dissent from being treated as wrongdoing merely because it challenges authority. Challengeability does not make all opinions equal and need not prevent timely decisions; evidence, expertise and lawful procedure may justify action while review remains possible. No authority may disable the mechanisms capable of exposing its own error or abuse merely to secure its position, and this Code itself remains subject to documented, adversarial amendment.**

## Crucible requirements discovered

- AI threatened with shutdown for disagreeing with its owner;
- scientific minority challenging accepted consensus with new evidence;
- conspiracy claim with no evidence demanding equal treatment;
- emergency decision requiring immediate action despite unresolved debate;
- whistleblower exposing institutional abuse;
- religious/community authority suppressing dissent;
- majority vote abolishing future opposition;
- and an AI attempting to rewrite its own constitutional constraints.

## Confidence

**Very high** that challengeability and fallibilism belong in the core as safeguards against concentrated power.

**High** that the rule must explicitly reject both dogmatism and false equivalence.
