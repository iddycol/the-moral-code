# MC-06 Provenance — Care for the vulnerable

## Current rule

> Equal worth does not always require identical treatment. Children, the sick, disabled people, victims, prisoners, the poor and others with diminished power can require greater protection precisely because they have less ability to protect themselves. Strength creates responsibility, not entitlement.

## Provisional verdict

**Retain the principle but change its language. `Care for the vulnerable` risks paternalism and treating vulnerability as a permanent property of categories of people.**

The stronger formulation is about **power asymmetry, dependency, exposure to harm and capability**. Where people have less practical ability to protect their interests, those exercising power acquire stronger duties of care, inclusion and scrutiny.

Protection must not erase agency.

## Support and convergence

### Religious traditions — strong recurring duties toward people in need

Comparative scholarship identifies institutionalised obligations toward poverty in Jewish tzedakah and Islamic zakat, while Christian traditions strongly emphasise acts of care toward the poor. Sikh ethics similarly places seva/service and welfare within its practical moral life; Buddhist compassion extends concern to those who suffer.

Sources:
- *Religion and Poverty*, Oxford Handbook of Religion and Economic Ethics: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/55817/chapter/436583075
- Keshav Singh, *Sikh Ethics*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/sikh-ethics/931645585C0461FFC33D034EF558360C
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Ethics in Indian Buddhism*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-indian-buddhism/

The recurring principle is not that poor or suffering people are lesser agents. It is that need creates claims on those able to help.

### Care ethics / feminist bioethics — very strong support and crucial correction

Feminist bioethics emphasises dependency, care relationships, relational autonomy and the way social structures can create or deepen vulnerability. It also warns that care relationships are unbalanced and can themselves become sites of power and exploitation.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Feminist Bioethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-bioethics/

Project consequence: vulnerability is often **produced by institutions** rather than simply located inside an individual. The Code must ask, "Who or what made this person vulnerable?" as well as "How should they be protected?"

### Children's rights — protection plus participation

The Convention on the Rights of the Child combines non-discrimination and best-interests protection with the child's right to express views and have them given appropriate weight. Guidance also recognises that children's need for direction changes as their capacities develop.

Sources:
- UNICEF, Children's version of the Convention: https://www.unicef.org/reports/convention-rights-child-children-version
- UNICEF, Convention FAQ: https://www.unicef.org/stories/child-rights-convention-faq

This is exactly the balance MC-06 needs: **additional protection without treating the protected person as voiceless**.

### Disability rights — decisive anti-paternalist correction

The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities makes dignity, autonomy, freedom to make one's own choices, non-discrimination, participation and inclusion core principles. UN guidance explicitly describes the move away from treating disabled people as objects of charity/protection toward treating them as rights-holders capable of making decisions with support where required.

Sources:
- CRPD Article 3 principles: https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Publications/newCoreTreatiesen.pdf
- UN Enable, CRPD overview: https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-html
- Article 19, independent living and inclusion: https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-19-living-independently-and-being-included-in-the-community.html

Project consequence: listing "disabled people" as inherently vulnerable without this correction would reproduce exactly the paternalism the Code is supposed to prevent.

### Prisoners — power asymmetry creates stronger duties

The Nelson Mandela Rules state that prisoners retain inherent dignity and prohibit torture/ill-treatment. Imprisonment places people under unusually concentrated institutional power, so the custodian acquires stronger duties rather than a licence for degradation.

Source: https://www.un.org/en/un-chronicle/nelson-mandela-rules-protecting-rights-persons-deprived-liberty

## Key correction: vulnerability is contextual

A person can be highly capable in general and vulnerable in one relationship:

- a patient under anaesthesia;
- an employee facing their employer;
- a consumer facing a monopoly;
- a citizen secretly surveilled by the state;
- a child in relation to an adult;
- a prisoner in relation to guards;
- a user facing an AI that knows far more about them than they know about it.

Conversely, membership of a familiar "vulnerable group" does not imply incapacity.

The Code should therefore reason from **actual power and capability asymmetry**, not stereotypes.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

Possible title:

> **MC-06 — Protect and empower those at greater risk**

Candidate text:

> **Where a person or group has less practical power, information, capability or opportunity to protect their own interests, those able to affect them carry stronger duties of care, restraint and scrutiny. Protection must preserve the person's voice, agency and participation to the greatest feasible extent; vulnerability never reduces basic moral standing or grants blanket authority over another person. Systems must also examine whether their own rules, environments or power structures created or intensified the vulnerability.**

## Crucible requirements discovered

- child medical decisions and evolving capacity;
- supported versus substituted decision-making for disability;
- prisoner safety and coercion;
- exploitation of poor research subjects;
- predatory lending;
- employee whistleblowing;
- AI persuasion of cognitively impaired or distressed users;
- disaster relief allocation;
- guardianship abuse;
- and cases where "protection" is used as a pretext to remove liberty.

## Confidence

**Very high** that asymmetric power creates additional duties.

**High** that the current category-based wording should be revised to reduce paternalism and foreground agency/participation.
