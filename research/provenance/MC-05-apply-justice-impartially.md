# MC-05 Provenance — Apply justice impartially

## Current rule

> Relevant equals should be treated equally. Irrelevant characteristics must not determine outcomes. Do not assign collective guilt. Do not punish a person for what another member of their group has done.

## Provisional verdict

**Retain strongly, with an important distinction: impartial public/coercive judgement does not require identical concern in every personal relationship.**

The evidence supports a broad prohibition on arbitrary status, collective guilt and self-serving exceptions. But several traditions reject the idea that justice always means identical treatment. Needs, roles, responsibilities and relationships can be morally relevant.

The correct target is **arbitrary partiality**, not all differentiation.

## Support and convergence

### Rawls — very strong procedural support

Rawls's original position is explicitly designed as a fair and impartial standpoint. The veil of ignorance removes knowledge of personal characteristics and social position so rules cannot be chosen merely to advantage whoever happens to hold power.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Original Position*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/original-position/

This remains a powerful operational test for the Moral Code: would the rule still look fair if the decision-maker did not know which affected position they would occupy?

### Islamic ethics — justice as central, but not synonymous with identical treatment

Contemporary scholarship describes justice as central to Islamic ethical and political thought and identifies universal human nature, dignity and just interhuman relations as important themes. Comparative scholarship also emphasises traditions of justice as **equivalence** rather than simple sameness: relevant differences in responsibility, role or circumstance may justify different treatment.

Sources:
- Abdulaziz Sachedina, *Natural Law and Ethical Necessity*: https://academic.oup.com/book/43872/chapter-abstract/370985742
- Raymond William Baker, *Justice in Islam*: https://academic.oup.com/book/43881
- Lawrence Rosen, *Islamic concepts of justice and injustice*: https://academic.oup.com/book/7126/chapter-abstract/151664293

Project consequence: the Code should demand a publicly defensible reason for difference, not mechanically identical outcomes.

### African ethics — justice and fairness within cooperative social life

The Stanford Encyclopedia survey identifies justice and fairness among the ethical ideas supporting social harmony and cooperative life in African moral traditions.

Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/

### Confucian ethics — a major corrective to simplistic impartiality

Confucian ethics explicitly recognises differentiated duties based on relationships. Analects 13.18 is a hard counterexample for public impartiality: family loyalty can be portrayed as taking precedence over reporting a relative's wrongdoing. The tradition therefore forces the Code to say when relational partiality is legitimate and when it corrupts public justice.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Chinese Ethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-chinese/

This is not a reason to abandon impartial justice. It demonstrates that **special personal duties** and **public/coercive authority** need different tests.

### Modern prisoner standards — equal basic standing survives wrongdoing

The UN Nelson Mandela Rules require prisoners to be treated with inherent dignity and applied without discrimination. This is a strong modern illustration of MC-05 working together with MC-01: guilt for a proved offence changes legitimate restrictions and consequences, but does not erase basic moral standing.

Source: United Nations, Nelson Mandela Rules overview and Rule 1/2: https://www.un.org/en/un-chronicle/nelson-mandela-rules-protecting-rights-persons-deprived-liberty

## Key distinction: impartiality is not sameness

A fair system may legitimately:

- give additional support to a person with a disability;
- impose greater duties on an office-holder with greater power;
- punish a proved offender while not punishing an innocent person;
- require parents to support their own children more than strangers;
- or allocate scarce treatment based on medically relevant need.

Those are differences based on relevant reasons.

An unfair system may not:

- punish a family, ethnicity or religion for one member's offence;
- give officials immunity from a rule they impose on others without a relevant justification;
- treat wealth, caste, race or popularity as evidence of guilt or entitlement;
- or change evidential standards depending on who benefits.

## Public power requires stronger impartiality than private affection

A parent may reasonably favour their child in giving birthday presents.

A judge may not favour their child in deciding a criminal case.

The more coercive and public the power, the stronger the requirement to exclude irrelevant partiality and conflicts of interest.

This should become an Interpretation Rule.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

The current principle is basically sound. Possible clarification:

> **Apply rules and evidence impartially wherever coercive or consequential power is exercised. Treat like cases alike and different cases differently only for reasons relevant to the legitimate purpose of the decision. Never assign collective guilt, and never make group identity a substitute for individual evidence. Personal relationships may create special duties, but they do not justify corrupting public judgement or denying another person's basic standing.**

## Crucible requirements discovered

- family loyalty versus reporting serious wrongdoing;
- racial profiling;
- collective punishment after terrorism/war;
- nepotism and conflicts of interest;
- rich versus poor defendants with identical evidence;
- disability accommodation versus formal identical treatment;
- scarce-resource allocation;
- and an AI giving favourable treatment to its owner or operator.

## Confidence

**Very high** that anti-arbitrariness, anti-collective-guilt and consistent evidential standards belong in the core.

**High** that the Code must explicitly distinguish impartial justice from simplistic identical treatment.
