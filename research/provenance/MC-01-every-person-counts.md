# MC-01 Provenance — Every person counts

## Current rule

> Every human being possesses moral worth that does not depend upon intelligence, wealth, health, usefulness, nationality, race, sex, beliefs, behaviour, popularity or power. No person may be treated simply as raw material for someone else's objective.

## Provisional verdict

**Retain the principle. Clarify its grounding and scope in v0.2.**

The first-pass evidence shows powerful convergence around moral standing that is not reducible to wealth, rank or usefulness. However, traditions ground that standing differently and do not all derive it from autonomous rational individuality. The principle should therefore remain metaphysically neutral and must not quietly equate dignity with Western individualism.

## Support and convergence

### Modern human-rights framework — strong support

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights begins from equal dignity and rights and expressly rejects distinctions based on race, sex, religion, social origin, property, birth and other status. It also prohibits slavery and torture.

Source: United Nations, *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*, Articles 1–5: https://www.un.org/en/node/124305

This is the closest modern legal expression of MC-01, but it is historically recent and therefore cannot by itself establish cross-cultural provenance.

### Kant — strong support with a narrower philosophical grounding

Kant's Humanity Formula requires treating humanity never merely as a means but also as an end. This strongly supports the Code's anti-instrumentalisation clause. Kant's grounding, however, is bound to rational agency rather than to a simple proposition that every biologically human life has identical moral status in every circumstance.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Kant's Moral Philosophy*, Humanity Formula: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/

Project consequence: retain the anti-instrumentalisation idea without making rational capacity the sole basis of moral worth; doing so would endanger infants, severely cognitively disabled people and others with diminished agency.

### African ethical traditions — strong support, different grounding

The Stanford Encyclopedia account of African ethics describes humanity, shared human welfare, brotherhood, compassion, reciprocity and social responsibility as central themes. It includes the Akan idea that a human being has value beyond material wealth and describes a universal human family extending beyond kin or ethnicity.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *African Ethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/

Crucial challenge: this tradition is typically **duty- and relationship-oriented**, not founded on the isolated rights-bearing individual. It therefore supports MC-01 while cautioning against deriving MC-01 from MC-03 autonomy.

### Sikh ethics — strong support

Recent scholarly reconstruction of Sikh ethics emphasises Oneness, equal worth, seva and institutions such as langar that intentionally erase social rank in shared practice. Cambridge's *Sikh Ethics* describes communal practices designed to embody egalitarianism; scholarship on langar similarly emphasises eating and serving across caste, sex and social position.

Sources:
- Keshav Singh, *Sikh Ethics*, Cambridge University Press (2026): https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/sikh-ethics/931645585C0461FFC33D034EF558360C
- Vesna Ratkovic, *Equality and seva — the Fundamentals of Sikhism that are accomplished in the langar* (2025): https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/482151

Contradiction/practice warning: scholarly work also records persistent caste and gender hierarchy in lived Sikh communities. Egalitarian doctrine must not be mistaken for uniform historical practice.

Source: Doris Jakobsh, *In Our Whole Society, There Is No Equality*: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/11/2/95

### Jewish ethics — strong/partial support with theological and communal grounding

Jewish ethical literature contains powerful traditions of neighbour-love, human dignity and the image of God. Scholarship also warns that Jewish conceptions of dignity are not simply interchangeable with liberal autonomy: some interpretations ground dignity in God, communal identity and commandments and can give those claims priority over personal liberty.

Sources:
- Lenn Goodman, *Love Thy Neighbour As Thyself*: https://academic.oup.com/book/36479
- Doron Shultziner, *A Jewish Conception of Human Dignity*: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9795.2006.00289.x
- Y. Michael Barilan, *From imago Dei in the Jewish-Christian traditions to human dignity in contemporary Jewish law*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19886523/

Project consequence: Jewish ethics supports human standing but also reinforces the need not to make autonomy synonymous with dignity.

### Teachings attributed to Jesus / Christian ethics — strong universalising strand, historical tension

The teachings attributed to Jesus extend moral concern beyond reciprocity: love of enemies, care for the stranger, and the Good Samaritan place moral demand on treatment of outsiders and adversaries. This is unusually strong support for the proposition that enemy status does not erase human moral standing.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Religion and Morality in Western Philosophy*, discussion of the Sermon on the Mount and Good Samaritan: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/religion-morality/

Tension: Christian institutions and Christian readings of scripture have also participated in slavery, persecution, war and hierarchy. The project must distinguish the ethical strand it inherits from the totality of Christian history.

### Islamic ethics — strong/partial support; historical contradiction

Contemporary scholarship on Islamic ethics identifies universal human nature, dignity, justice and reason as important bases for just interhuman relations, including relations across faith communities.

Sources:
- Abdulaziz Sachedina, *Islamic Ethics: Fundamental Aspects of Human Conduct* (Oxford, 2022): https://academic.oup.com/book/43872
- Sachedina, *Natural Law and Ethical Necessity*: https://academic.oup.com/book/43872/chapter-abstract/370985742

Contradiction: historical Islamic legal and political traditions, like Christian, Jewish and other historical societies, accommodated slavery and status hierarchy. Modern abolitionist Islamic interpretation is historically developed rather than a simple description of all prior jurisprudence.

Source: Haroon Bashir, *Slavery, Abolition, and Islam* (Oxford, 2025): https://academic.oup.com/book/59288

### Buddhism — partial support; different moral ontology

Buddhist ethics strongly emphasises compassion, lovingkindness, avoiding harmful action and concern for sentient beings. It does not naturally begin from a Western concept of intrinsic individual rights or permanent selves.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Ethics in Indian Buddhism*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-indian-buddhism/

Project consequence: Buddhism supports broad moral concern but cautions against claiming that the exact phrase "inherent individual human worth" is a literal cross-tradition concept.

### Jainism — supports and challenges MC-01

Jain ethics makes non-violence central and extends moral concern far beyond humans to living beings. That supports the rejection of disposable life but challenges a purely anthropocentric moral constitution.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Jaina Philosophy*: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2023/entries/jaina-philosophy/

Project consequence: MC-01 can remain human-focused as a constitutional protection, but the Code must not imply that only humans possess moral standing. MC-02 and MC-09 should carry broader sentient/ecological duties.

### Confucian ethics — partial support with role hierarchy tension

Confucian ren includes caring for others and sympathetic understanding, but Confucian ethics is deeply relational and historically role-differentiated. It does not map neatly onto an abstract equality rule detached from social relationships.

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Chinese Ethics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-chinese/

Project consequence: equal moral standing must be distinguished from identical duties or identical treatment in every relationship.

### Hindu traditions — important contradiction to confront

Hindu ethical traditions contain rich concepts of dharma and later reformist universalism, but caste and gender hierarchy are not merely accidental external practices. Recent Oxford scholarship argues that caste hierarchy has deep religious/social roots within Hindu traditions, while reform movements have contested those roots.

Sources:
- M.V. Nadkarni, *Ethics in Hinduism*: https://academic.oup.com/book/12339/chapter-abstract/161899248
- *Hinduism and Caste System*, Oxford Handbook of Caste: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/55829/chapter-abstract/437503600
- Kim Knott, *Challenges to Hinduism: women and dalits*: https://academic.oup.com/book/901/chapter-abstract/135487108

Project consequence: we cannot claim that equality is simply "what Hinduism teaches". Instead, MC-01 must be justified as part of the convergent core that survives rejection of inherited status hierarchy.

## Key contradiction discovered

The project began with the language of **inherent worth**. That wording is defensible, but its philosophical basis is not genuinely common:

- Kant: rational humanity/end-in-itself.
- Jewish/Christian strands: image of God / neighbour love.
- Islamic strands: universal human nature and divine justice.
- Sikh ethics: Oneness.
- African ethics: shared humanity and relational duty.
- Buddhism/Jainism: compassion/non-harm toward sentient/living beings.
- modern rights law: equal dignity and rights.

The common conclusion is stronger than any single metaphysical explanation:

> **No human may lose basic moral standing merely because another actor judges them less useful, less intelligent, less powerful, less worthy, or a member of a disfavoured group.**

That may be the more robust v0.2 formulation.

## Proposed v0.2 amendment candidate

Current opening:

> Every human being possesses moral worth that does not depend upon intelligence, wealth, health, usefulness, nationality, race, sex, beliefs, behaviour, popularity or power.

Candidate:

> **Every human being has basic moral standing that cannot be erased by intelligence, wealth, health, usefulness, nationality, race, sex, belief, behaviour, popularity, status or power. Differences between people may create different needs, responsibilities or consequences, but they do not make a person disposable.**

Why this may be better:

- avoids pretending all traditions share one theory of "intrinsic worth";
- makes non-disposability operational;
- allows legitimate role/responsibility differences without allowing caste-like moral hierarchy;
- protects people with diminished rational agency;
- leaves non-human moral standing to MC-02/MC-09 rather than denying it.

## Confidence

**High** that a non-disposability/equal-basic-standing principle belongs in the Moral Core.

**Medium** that the current philosophical language is the best cross-tradition expression.
